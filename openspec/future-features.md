# Future product features

This is a backlog of desired features outside currently scoped implementation changes. It remains within OpenSpec, the repository's planning home. Approval of a direction is not approval of an unspecified implementation; promote an item into a dedicated change before building it.

## Multiple acceptable known answers

Status: desired future feature, recorded 2026-10-06. Ownership: question/answer-key authoring and grading, outside `add-worker-reputation`.

Allow an author to mark more than one answer as acceptable. For a single-choice sentiment question, both “slightly positive” and “positive” could be valid. This means either answer passes; it is different from requiring the worker to select both options in a multiple-choice answer.

The future design should define typed acceptable-answer sets, validation, matching, and correction/version semantics. Reputation should consume the grading outcome when this capability exists, without owning an alternative answer representation or changing score weights merely to support it.

## Question-level scoring controls

Status: approved future direction, recorded 2026-10-06. Requires question-contract design before implementation; outside the current reputation change.

Let authors control which questions contribute to quality scoring, especially peer agreement. A form containing both factual classification and personal preference should be able to evaluate agreement on the factual question while excluding the preference question. Decide the exact controls and their relationship to organization/project defaults in the future change; do not add a general formula system.

That change must address published question immutability and activation-time resolution. It will also require coordinated changes to reputation: select eligible questions before computing signal counts and scores, apply minimum-evidence rules to the selected observations, exclude disabled questions from enforcement, explain exclusions, and rebuild affected assessments under the correct pinned question contract. Existing active projects must retain their frozen behavior. The [reputation design](changes/add-worker-reputation/design.md#future-question-capabilities) records this dependency.

## Spatial grading for bounding boxes and segmentation

Status: approved future direction, recorded 2026-10-07. Ownership: question types, reference-answer authoring, and annotation comparison; outside the current reputation change.

Partial credit was approved on 2026-10-08. Award credit for correctly annotated parts of an answer rather than failing the entire question because one object or region is wrong. Missing/extra objects, incorrect labels, and inadequate spatial overlap must still reduce the result. Retain a breakdown so authorized reviewers can distinguish the correctly annotated parts from the errors.

For bounding boxes, the approved question-level formula is `correct objects / (correct objects + missed objects + extra annotations)`, the object-count Jaccard metric. Match each reference box to at most one submitted box and each submitted box to at most one reference box; a correct match requires the same label and sufficient spatial overlap. Unmatched references are missed objects and unmatched submitted boxes are extra annotations, so a wrong label contributes both a miss and an extra annotation. With three reference objects, two correct and one omitted earns `2/3`; two correct and one incorrectly labeled earns `2/4`; three correct plus one extra earns `3/4`. Reputation averages eligible per-question fractional grades once its actual evaluated-question minimum is met, counting each question once regardless of its object count. See [CVAT's object-count metrics](https://docs.cvat.ai/docs/qa-analytics/auto-qa/#quality-target-metrics). The default object-overlap threshold remains to be specified.

Bounding-box overlap matching was approved on 2026-10-08. Use Intersection over Union (IoU): shared area divided by the total area covered by either box, counting shared area once. A same-label pair qualifies when its IoU meets or exceeds one configurable minimum for the project; exact positions and dimensions need not match. A qualifying object earns full object credit in the count formula, rather than multiplying that credit by its IoU. Partial question credit comes from the fraction of correctly matched objects. The discussed 70% threshold is an illustration, not an approved default. Choose a suitable threshold using representative acceptable/unacceptable annotations, including small objects, and preserve it through the future capability's frozen project contract. See [CVAT's overlap comparison settings](https://docs.cvat.ai/docs/qa-analytics/auto-qa/#annotation-quality-settings).

Object pairing was approved on 2026-10-08 for bounding boxes and separately outlined segmentation objects. Consider only same-label pairs that meet the project overlap threshold, then choose a one-to-one pairing with the greatest number of valid matches. Among pairings with the same match count, maximize total IoU; resolve exact ties consistently, independent of annotation presentation or drawing order. Each reference and each submitted annotation may participate in at most one pair, and unpaired submitted annotations, including duplicates, remain extras. For example, if submitted annotation 1 can match reference A or B and annotation 2 can match only A, pair 2 with A and 1 with B rather than losing a valid match by assigning 1 to A first. Category-based segmentation has no object-pairing step. The future annotation contract must define the deterministic final tie-break and retain the resulting comparison breakdown for authorized reviewers.

Segmentation scoring was approved on 2026-10-08. For separately outlined objects (instance segmentation), use the same one-to-one, same-label matching and object-count formula as bounding boxes. Calculate IoU over the actual polygon regions or painted mask pixels and use the project's configurable minimum overlap to identify correct object matches; a qualifying match earns full object credit. Missing and extra objects reduce the question score.

For category-based painting (semantic segmentation), calculate pixel IoU separately for each category present in either the reference or the worker answer, then average those category scores equally. Use each category's continuous overlap as partial credit, without converting it to a pass/fail match. A completely missed or incorrectly added category has zero overlap; a category absent from both answers contributes nothing to the average. Exclude unlabeled background so a large untouched area cannot hide poor annotations. For example, 90% car overlap and 70% road overlap earns 80% question credit. Each evaluated segmentation question remains one reputation observation, regardless of its categories, objects, or pixels. The future question contract must distinguish separately identified objects from category-level painting. Detailed annotation representation/validation remains undecided. See [HumanSignal's polygon and mask overlap metrics](https://docs.humansignal.com/guide/agreement_metrics#iou-for-other-region-types).

Empty-answer grading was approved on 2026-10-08 for all three spatial styles. A trusted reference explicitly containing no objects or labeled regions and a valid worker answer explicitly submitting none earns full question credit; this supplies one evaluated question rather than an undefined zero-denominator score. If only the reference or only the explicit worker answer is empty, the question earns zero credit. A skipped, missing, untouched, or unfinished answer remains unscored, not a successful empty answer. The future question/answer contract must distinguish a submitted valid empty annotation from an unanswered question. No supplied reference is not an empty reference: without a trusted reference, known-answer grading is unavailable. These rules do not manufacture human approval or spatial peer consensus.

Spatial peer comparison was approved on 2026-10-08. Exclude the assessed worker, duplicate votes from a peer, skips, and absent answers under reputation's existing exact task/question/input eligibility. Compare eligible peer answers using the same spatial question-score formulas above. Retain every actual submitted peer answer tied for the highest arithmetic mean similarity to the other eligible peers, excluding each candidate's self-comparison from its mean. Worker reputation must not influence representative selection or vote weights. Do not average geometry, generate a trusted key, choose a canonical collected answer, or rewrite submissions/exports.

Retain completed-task eligibility and the existing response-count-dependent peer coverage requirement. Before scoring the assessed worker, require at least the assessed scope's consensus fraction of all eligible other-worker answers to be sufficiently similar to each retained representative; the initial consensus fraction remains 80%. Each representative is one of those eligible peers and supplies no extra vote. Use every eligible peer when selecting representatives and checking consensus, not a subset chosen to pass. Missing coverage or insufficient consensus around any representative leaves the question unscored and supplies no worker minimum-evidence credit. Once consensus is established around every representative, compare the assessed answer with each using its actual fractional spatial question score, then average those grades into one worker observation, preserving the existing repeated-evidence minimum and known-answer precedence. Preserve independent project/organization/global assessment settings and source-project agreement exclusions. Whole-answer similarity is distinct from individual-object IoU and from the fraction of peers required for consensus. See [HumanSignal's continuous comparison and thresholded consensus distinction](https://docs.humansignal.com/guide/stats).

Representative-answer tie handling was approved on 2026-10-08. Detect exact ties in unrounded candidate mean scores, keeping every tied best representative from the eligible distinct-peer pool. Check the full-pool consensus condition independently around every tied answer; do not select a convenient tied answer or discard a tied answer that lacks consensus. If every tied answer qualifies, the worker's question grade is the arithmetic mean of its comparison grades against those answers, such as 100% against A and 80% against B yielding 90% credit. This remains one evaluated-question observation. A unique best representative is the one-answer case of the same rule. Averaging grades does not merge annotation geometry or generate a reference, and the assessed worker's answer never affects which representatives are retained. This is distinct from the deterministic tie-break for pairing individual objects, where the match count and total IoU are already fixed.

The whole-answer peer similarity cutoff was approved on 2026-10-08, initially 80% in project, organization, and global assessments. A peer counts as agreeing with the representative when their spatial question comparison score meets or exceeds this cutoff. Support organization defaults and project overrides, retaining frozen active-project settings; global assessment uses its independently controlled platform cutoff and organization assessment uses its own effective organization setting. Neither source-project overrides nor another scope's cutoff configure broader assessments. The similarity cutoff and consensus fraction are independent controls even though both initially equal 80%: with ten eligible other workers, at least eight must have comparison scores of at least 80% under the initial settings. For a representative with five objects, four correct and one omitted scores 80% and qualifies, four correct and one incorrectly labeled scores `4/6` and does not qualify, and five correct plus one extra scores `5/6` and qualifies. For category-based segmentation, apply the cutoff to mean category IoU. This cutoff tests peer consensus only; after consensus is established, an assessed worker with a 60% comparison score earns a 60% observation, not zero or full credit. Preserve fractional values and inclusive boundaries without display rounding when selecting representatives, checking agreement, and retaining worker grades. This approval does not set the individual-object IoU default.

Allow organizations to supply trusted reference boxes or segmentation masks and automatically compare worker annotations with reasonable tolerance. Evaluate object labels, overlapping regions, and missing/extra objects; identical coordinates or pixels should not be required. IoU is the approved overlap measure for bounding boxes and segmentation, with different approved question-score aggregation for separate objects and category painting. Overlap alone is not a complete quality rule, and no universal acceptance threshold is approved. Producing a reference once can support grading many workers' answers to the same inputs; it does not guarantee that ordinary allocation delivers those questions to every worker.

The spatial change must extend reputation's grading-input, quality-aggregation, and reporting contracts to preserve fractional credit instead of rounding it to a full pass or failure. Keep the worker's actual evaluated-question count separate from earned credit and annotation counts; multiple objects in one question must not manufacture additional worker evidence. Preserve the equal per-question weighting when pooling scoped evidence. Specify how partial grades affect known-answer and peer metrics, explanations, corrections, and any configured access threshold. The current reputation change retains its approved binary matching for supported non-spatial families; this direction does not change selection/ranking matching, quality weights, or evidence minima.

Specify annotation representations, published-contract validation, object matching, acceptable overlap, and correction/version behavior alongside the relevant question/media capability. The same type-specific comparison can support peer agreement, which remains weaker evidence than matching a trusted reference. Coordinate reputation eligibility, observation counts, provenance, and recalculation when this capability is implemented; keep geometry outside the reputation calculation and do not create a generic grader framework in advance. Free-text semantic grading remains a separate undecided capability. See the [reputation integration boundary](changes/add-worker-reputation/design.md#future-question-capabilities) and [HumanSignal's spatial-comparison metrics](https://docs.humansignal.com/guide/agreement_metrics).

## Review sampling and budgets

Status: approved future direction, recorded 2026-10-07. Ownership: answer-review workflow; outside `add-worker-reputation`.

Let organizations choose a project review budget or sample size without committing to a review quota per worker. Include a random sample for investigating overall output quality and instruction problems, with additional targeted investigation when needed. Record how responses were selected, distinguishing routine/random sampling from suspicion-driven review. An illustrative budget such as one hundred reviews across three hundred workers is not a guarantee of statistical confidence or evidence for every individual worker.

Specify selection, authorization, budget limits, and how sampling interacts with frozen automatic/manual review modes, effective verdicts, result visibility, and corrections. Do not automatically treat unsampled answers as human-approved, or imply that a targeted review queue estimates the acceptance rate of all submissions. Reviews must remain attributable to actual submitted answers and workers; a project-wide sample rate cannot establish an unreviewed worker's quality or satisfy their minimum evidence.

The [reputation contract](changes/add-worker-reputation/specs/worker-reputation/spec.md#requirement-human-review-is-supplementary-worker-evidence) specifies consumption of actual substantive reviews, supplementary human-review scoring unselected by default, and explicit opt-in and insufficient-evidence rules. This future capability supplies sampling workflow and provenance rather than a second reputation scorer. [Labelbox's quality strategy](https://labelbox.com/guides/how-to-create-a-quality-strategy-for-your-data-labeling-project/) provides a precedent for reducing manual review to a sample while using benchmarks and applicable consensus.

## Worker explanations and disputes

Status: approved future direction, explicitly deferred on 2026-10-06; outside `add-worker-reputation`.

Give workers understandable explanations of their reputation and restrictions, and a way to ask an authorized organization reviewer to reconsider potentially incorrect evidence or an unfair outcome. For example, a worker could report ambiguous instructions or an incorrect expected answer. Keep hidden answer keys, peer answers, and private organization evidence protected.

The future change must define what workers can see, how they submit a dispute, how reviewers respond, and how the resolution connects to existing key correction, recalculation, and reinstatement. Detailed workflow and response-time rules remain undecided. The current reputation change retains organization-side explanations and recovery actions; it does not include worker-facing explanation or dispute workflows.

## Organization and project score weights

Status: deferred on 2026-10-07; outside the current reputation change.

Allow organizations to choose signal weights, with project overrides if needed. The initial reputation implementation keeps weights centrally defined in application code and supplies them explicitly to the shared calculation, making developer adjustments straightforward without exposing customer controls. Other approved organization/project settings remain in scope.

Before exposing weights, specify valid combinations, treatment of missing evidence, activation-time freezing, and how local weighting remains independent of platform-controlled global reputation. Reuse the existing calculation and effective-configuration model rather than introducing customer-authored formulas or another scoring engine.
